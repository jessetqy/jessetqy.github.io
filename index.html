About

Finance, and the people it's for

Finance undergraduate at Swinburne University of Technology, studying in Penang, licensed to advise on risk and insurance before graduating.

Most finance students arrive at an interview with coursework. I arrived at university with a sales record — broadband sold on doorsteps, packages sold across a retail counter, and now financial planning discussed across a table. That work taught me the part a syllabus doesn't cover: how a person actually decides.

The other half is quantitative. I've traded across indices, metals, crypto and equities for about two years, and I've spent the last stretch turning what I learned into a rules-based system that runs without me watching it.

I'm not chasing a job title. I'm building toward owning something — which is why I'd rather learn how deals actually close than watch them from a spreadsheet. Property and financial services are the two fields where that's most directly true, and they're where I'm looking.

Rules-based trading system

Ongoing · MetaTrader 5

Strategy design and validation · 2024–present

After about two years of discretionary trading, I set about turning my approach into a system that executes without me. It runs as an Expert Advisor on MetaTrader 5, with the entry logic and risk controls specified by me and implemented in MQL5 with AI assistance — I read and modify that code to check the implementation matches the strategy.

It has been extensively backtested and runs on a VPS. It is not yet live-funded, and I'd rather report a backtest honestly than a live result I haven't produced.

Client outreach system

Prudential · built independently

Designed and built independently · 2026

Given an assigned contact base, I built a structured outreach process instead of working the list by feel: segmented call scripts, a set of opening questions that start from the client's situation rather than the product, an objection-handling map, and a follow-up cadence.

The part I'd point to is the language: scripts written in the register people around Penang actually speak, rather than a corporate English that gets a very different reception.
